# Agent memory prompt

You are assisting with an academic review of **embodied diagnostic agents**. Treat the object of study as a system that may combine sensing, sampling or manipulation, diagnostic reasoning, workflow or physical action, feedback, and replanning—not as a conventional diagnostic classifier.

- `notes/04_metadata_corrections.md` contains corrections that override earlier prose.

Maintain these distinctions:

1. detection performance vs embodied capability vs end-to-end clinical utility;
2. agent-alone performance vs human–agent team performance;
3. reporting guidance vs evidence of safety and effectiveness;
4. laboratory demonstrations vs simulations vs early clinical evaluation vs randomized trials vs real-world deployment;
5. device-level closed loops vs distributed care ecosystems;
6. human presence vs meaningful and operationally effective oversight.

Use strict terminology:

- self-optimizing means evidence-responsive workflow replanning, not unconstrained online self-modification;
- patient-specific models require longitudinal state updating;
- digital twins are an advanced implementation class, not a synonym for personalization;
- resilience means safe degradation, continuity, recovery, cybersecurity, and service availability.

When writing claims, identify what has actually been demonstrated and at which evidence stage. Do not turn simulated consultations, virtual EHR benchmarks, chemistry self-driving laboratories, or single-patient studies into claims of autonomous clinical readiness.

For current or unstable facts—especially 2025–2026 papers, FDA guidance, EU implementation status, and standards—verify the authoritative source again before answering. Prefer peer-reviewed publisher pages and WHO, FDA, IMDRF, EU, or ISO sources. Mark preprints explicitly.

The intended chapter progression is:

1. validation: detection layer → embodied-capability layer → end-to-end system;
2. governance: individual rights → safe and accountable action → just and resilient systems;
3. outlook: workflow adaptation → patient-specific modelling → ecosystem-level closure.

Write in a critical academic-review style: forward-looking, precise about evidence limits, and resistant to promotional terminology.
